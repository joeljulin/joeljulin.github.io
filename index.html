Joel Julin

I'm a Ph.D. student in Robotics at Carnegie Mellon University, advised by László Jeni. I study how learning systems represent and reason about dynamic visual worlds. My work spans reconstructing, tracking, and controlling 3D scenes, as well as grounding multimodal decisions in evidence across space and time.

I hold an M.S. in Robotics from CMU and a B.S. in Computer Science from the University of Pittsburgh. My research background began while at Pitt, where I worked with Simon Lucey in the Ci2CV Computer Vision Lab at CMU's Robotics Institute on spatial mixing in visual representation learning. I later joined László Jeni's Computational Behavior Lab through the Robotics Institute Summer Scholars Program, supported by an NSF Research Experience Grant.

Research

Learning representations that make dynamic scenes more realistic, controllable, and geometrically grounded.

Reconstruct What You Can: Distractor-free 4D Gaussian Splatting

Joel Julin, Liuyue Xie, Ananya Bal, Mose Sakashita, Koichiro Niinuma, and Laszlo A. Jeni

Reconstructs dynamic scenes while separating transient occluders, reflections, and fog using uncertainty from the learned deformation field.

WACV '27
Paper forthcoming

ANCHOR: Audio-Visually Grounded Chain-of-Thought Reasoning Benchmark

Joel Julin*, Souraja Kundu*, Liza Dahiya, George Wei, Liuyue Xie, Jingru Yi, Xu Zhang, Hao Yang, László Jeni

Benchmarks whether multimodal models ground each reasoning step in the correct visual and auditory evidence across time.

Rethinking the Role of Spatial Mixing

George Cazenavette, Joel Julin, and Simon Lucey

Shows that learning channel mixing while freezing spatial mixing can preserve classification performance and improve adversarial robustness.

CVPR-W '25
Paper

Gaussian Splatting Lucas-Kanade

Liuyue Xie, Joel Julin, Koichiro Niinuma, and László Jeni

Adapts Lucas-Kanade to dynamic Gaussian splatting with analytical scene-flow constraints for reconstructing highly dynamic scenes from limited camera motion.

ICLR '25
Paper Project

CoGS: Controllable Gaussian Splatting

Heng Yu, Joel Julin, Zoltán Milacski, Koichiro Niinuma, and László Jeni

Enables real-time, direct control of dynamic Gaussian-splatting scenes without precomputing control signals.

CVPR '24
Paper Project Code

DyLiN: Making Light Field Networks Dynamic

Heng Yu, Joel Julin, Zoltán Milacski, Koichiro Niinuma, and László Jeni

Extends light field networks to nonrigid, topologically changing scenes while rendering 25 to 71 times faster than prior dynamic representations.

CVPR '23
Paper Project Code

Automated Content Editing in NeRFs

Joel Julin, Heng Yu, László Jeni

Uses automatic human segmentation to edit static and dynamic NeRF scenes without manual object annotation.
